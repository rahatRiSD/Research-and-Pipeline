Abstract

Bangla natural language inference remains difficult not because encoders are weak, but because the supervision they read is structurally noisy. Two properties of Bangla are responsible. First, compound predicates — a lexical verb followed by a light or vector verb, as in কিনে এনেছে — split the semantic head from the inflected syntactic head, so an encoder conditioned on the surface head attends to the wrong token. Second, corpora assembled from web text carry inconsistent Unicode normalisation, stray zero-width joiners, and unsegmented code-switched spans, all of which silently corrupt tokenisation before the model ever sees a sentence.

We address both. We define a CPC (Compound Predicate Construction) dependency-head workflow that identifies compound-predicate spans, separates the lexical head from the syntactic head, and conditions the NLI classifier on the lexical head. We pair it with an automated corpus-repair pipeline that normalises script, segments code-switched spans, and re-attaches dependency arcs inside repaired spans. To measure whether a repaired corpus is actually usable rather than merely cleaner, we introduce BCSF (Bangla Corpus Structural Fidelity), a weighted composite of head-attachment accuracy, inter-annotator agreement, code-switch consistency, and orthographic normalisation.

Evaluated with BanglaBERT and BanglishBERT against multilingual baselines, CPC conditioning improves accuracy consistently, with the largest gains on premise–hypothesis pairs whose inference hinges on a compound predicate. The demo below runs the full path — repair, parse, infer, score — on sentences you type.

Contributions

Four contributions, each with a corresponding module in the live demo.

C1

CPC dependency-head workflow

A deterministic procedure that detects compound-predicate spans, labels V1 as the lexical head and V2 as the syntactic head, and re-points the classifier's head embedding at V1.

C2

Automated corpus-repair pipeline

Five ordered stages from Unicode normalisation to leakage removal, each reporting what it changed, so repair is auditable rather than a black box.

C3

BCSF, a corpus-fidelity score

A single interpretable number with a published weighting, so two versions of a corpus — or two repair configurations — can be compared directly.

C4

Bangla / Banglish NLI evaluation

Monolingual and code-mixed evaluation of BanglaBERT and BanglishBERT with and without CPC conditioning, including a compound-predicate-only subset.

3

Method

The system is a single forward path. Raw text is repaired before it is parsed, parsed before heads are selected, and only then encoded — so every downstream component sees text whose structure has already been made explicit.
